# Cybersecurity Emergency Response Training

## Organization

**Internee.pk**

## Purpose

The purpose of this training is to teach Internee.pk staff how to recognize, report, and respond to cybersecurity incidents, especially phishing and ransomware attacks.

---

## 1. Training Objectives

After completing this training, staff should be able to:

- Identify common cybersecurity threats.
- Recognize phishing emails and malicious attachments.
- Report suspicious activity quickly.
- Follow the correct emergency response procedure.
- Understand what to do during a ransomware incident.
- Protect company data and systems.
- Avoid actions that could make an incident worse.

---

## 2. Common Cybersecurity Threats

### Phishing

Phishing is an attempt to trick users into revealing information or opening malicious content.

Common warning signs:

- Unexpected emails.
- Urgent or threatening language.
- Suspicious links.
- Unknown attachments.
- Requests for passwords or sensitive information.
- Spelling or formatting inconsistencies.
- Fake login pages.

### Ransomware

Ransomware is malware that can make files or systems unavailable, commonly through encryption, and may demand payment from the victim.

Possible warning signs:

- Files cannot be opened.
- Files have unexpected extensions.
- A ransom note appears.
- Many files change suddenly.
- Security software generates an alert.
- Systems behave unusually.

---

## 3. Emergency Response Procedure

Internee.pk staff should follow:

# STOP → ISOLATE → REPORT

### STOP

Stop interacting with the suspicious email, attachment, link, or application.

Do not continue clicking or entering credentials.

### ISOLATE

If instructed by the IT/security team:

- Disconnect the affected computer from the network.
- Do not reconnect it without authorization.
- Do not connect external storage devices.

### REPORT

Immediately report the incident to the designated IT/security team.

Include:

- Your name/team.
- Approximate time of the incident.
- What happened.
- Which email, link, or file was involved.
- Any unusual message or screen displayed.

---

## 4. What Employees Should NOT Do

Employees must not:

- Delete suspicious emails or files if security needs them as evidence.
- Try to remove malware themselves.
- Reconnect an isolated computer.
- Forward suspicious attachments to coworkers.
- Share passwords.
- Attempt to negotiate with attackers.
- Hide an incident from management or IT.
- Use unauthorized recovery tools.

---

## 5. Ransomware Emergency Scenario

### Scenario

An employee receives an email claiming that their account requires immediate verification.

The email contains an attachment.

The employee opens it and then notices that several files are becoming inaccessible.

### Expected Employee Response

1. Stop using the affected system.
2. Do not open additional files.
3. Follow IT/security instructions for network isolation.
4. Report the incident immediately.
5. Do not delete files or ransom notes.
6. Do not attempt unauthorized recovery.
7. Wait for instructions from the incident-response team.

---

## 6. Security Team Response

After receiving the report, the security team should:

1. Confirm the incident.
2. Isolate affected systems.
3. Determine the scope of the incident.
4. Protect unaffected systems.
5. Disable or secure compromised accounts.
6. Preserve logs and other relevant evidence.
7. Identify the initial attack vector.
8. Remove malicious access.
9. Verify backups.
10. Restore clean systems.
11. Monitor restored systems.
12. Document the incident.

---

## 7. Password and MFA Awareness

Employees should:

- Use strong, unique passwords.
- Never share passwords.
- Never approve an unexpected MFA request.
- Report suspicious MFA notifications.
- Use MFA wherever provided.
- Never enter credentials into suspicious links.

---

## 8. Backup Awareness

Staff should understand that backups are important for ransomware recovery.

The organization should maintain:

- Regular backups.
- Protected backups.
- Offline or otherwise isolated backup copies.
- Tested restoration procedures.

Employees should never disable or modify backup systems without authorization.

---

## 9. Security Awareness Exercise

### Exercise 1 — Phishing

Employees receive a simulated email:

> **URGENT: Your account will be suspended. Open the attached document to verify your account.**

Employees must identify:

- The urgency/threat.
- The unexpected attachment.
- The request to take immediate action.
- The possibility that the message is malicious.

Correct action:

**Do not open the attachment. Report the message to the security team.**

---

## 10. Exercise 2 — Ransomware

A simulated workstation displays a ransomware warning and test files become inaccessible.

Employees should:

- Stop interacting with the workstation.
- Follow the isolation procedure.
- Contact IT/security.
- Preserve evidence.
- Wait for further instructions.

The exercise must use a **controlled environment and dummy data**. Real ransomware must never be deployed against production systems.
